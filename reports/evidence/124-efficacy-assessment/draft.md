# AgentLang efficacy evidence synthesis

Reviewed 2026-10-08 against the current roadmap, approved structural requirements, and reports 064, 096–102, 109–122. Trial counts below belong to their own protocols; they should not be pooled.

The evidence supports feasibility: fresh agents have discovered and reused prior typed words, produced persistent tested definitions, repaired seeded business defects, and used a newly exposed effect assertion to reject a known bad write. It does not establish that AgentLang edits are more reliable or cheaper than a well-designed typed repository. Feature implementation and local test passes are not agent-efficacy results.

## Structural requirements and research status

The approved structural plan in [docs/STRUCTURAL-CORRECTNESS.md](../../docs/STRUCTURAL-CORRECTNESS.md) is being delivered in slices. Locally validated implementation evidence includes checked and total ratio arithmetic ([110](../../reports/110-structural-correctness.md)), closed enums and exhaustive matches ([112](../../reports/112-closed-domain-states.md)), exact target-scoped effect-count assertions ([113](../../reports/113-effect-count-assertions.md)), finite input/return observations for library qualification ([116](../../reports/116-finite-library-coverage.md)), a populated delivery contract ([118](../../reports/118-populated-delivery-contract.md)), whole-record validators in the verified interpreter and supported LLVM constructor ([119](../../reports/119-record-construction-invariants.md)), and improved Flow/2 validator help ([121](../../reports/121-record-validation-guidance.md)). These implementation checks do not by themselves show that agents discover or benefit from the features.

Adoption evidence is mixed and bounded: report 115 is a positive effect-assertion probe; report 117 shows one agent responding to finite-coverage feedback; reports 120 and 122 show that validator ownership and help-version selection still affect qualification. None compares AgentLang with a conventional workflow on the same repeated task.

The roadmap and approved requirements still identify provider-state assertions, broader module boundaries and dependency-closure/requalification rules, test-local typed dictionary overrides with cleanup, native enum execution, and broader deterministic stateful properties as pending. Effect counts alone do not establish final state or ordering. Cartesian input coverage and MC/DC remain decisions to evaluate, not adopted requirements. Report 119 also marks validated-record returns with finite projections as currently unqualifiable without a proven valid-value domain; the raw C allocator remains a trusted internal boundary. These are implementation limits, not evidence that the full research goal is complete.

Reports 096 and 098 are memory/async design candidates. They record no runtime implementation or performance result. Agent-edit trials do not support production throughput, memory-safety, or native-runtime claims. Report 100's Flow/2 implementation validation likewise is not an actor result; report 111 is the inspected current-syntax comparison, and it remains one actor per condition without a superiority result.

## Highest-value next experiment

First finish the already pending report-122 same-task help exposure check: keep task, runtime, seed and oracle fixed; put the explicit request `{"op":"help","topic":"define","syntaxVersion":2}` in the primer; and verify that the response identifies syntax version 2. Score behavior, validator-owned tests, library maturity and reload separately. Record it as a bounded usability observation, not as a causal estimate from reports 120 and 122.

After that probe, prioritize a small comparative efficacy study over further preparatory implementation or resuming the deferred 004 harness. Use current Flow/2 on an unfamiliar business rule or refactor with an independent expected-value oracle. Align the task/tool contract and helper roles across retained, reset-rich and conventional conditions; make presence of the previously accepted abstraction the planned vocabulary treatment. Do not repeat the pricing defect. Give fresh actors the same task without naming the target operation. Seed a plausible wrong implementation or passing-but-wrong expectations so the study can observe diagnosis and test repair, not just clean-task completion. Replicate each condition: a single actor per arm should remain explicitly exploratory.

Score independently whether code and expectations are corrected, unrelated behavior is preserved, the right prior helper is actually discovered and called, the resulting definition qualifies honestly, and mutations/regressions are rejected. Preserve all starts, failed edits and acceptance evidence. Use existing scoring and trace tools where possible; keep protocol exchanges separate from model-token or time claims. If compactness is an outcome, measure comparable retrieved context and provider usage where available rather than inferring cost from exchange counts. This is the next test of the primary hypothesis, not completion of the broader research goal.
